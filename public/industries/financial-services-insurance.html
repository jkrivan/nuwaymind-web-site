Business reality

The procurement model must reflect the specific pressure points of Financial Services & Insurance.

Financial services spend is heavily weighted toward technology, outsourcing, advisory, claims services, data services, facilities and professional services. Many suppliers have access to sensitive systems, data or critical business processes.

Procurement must therefore work with Risk, Compliance, Legal, IT Security, Data Protection, Finance and business owners before commitments are made, not after contracts are signed.

A mature model gives management visibility of third-party risk, concentration, contract obligations, regulatory evidence, renewal exposure and spend leakage across business units.

Main industry challenges

Where procurement typically loses control, value or management visibility.

Third-party risk must be assessed before commitment

Suppliers may process personal data, access systems, support regulated operations or perform outsourced activities. Risk assessment after contract signature is too late; procurement must trigger security, privacy, resilience and compliance review early.

SaaS and cloud spend grows outside central control

Business teams can subscribe to tools quickly, creating shadow IT, data exposure, duplicate licenses and weak renewal negotiation. Procurement must connect technology buying with IT security and contract review.

Outsourcing governance requires ongoing evidence

Critical or important outsourced services need documented ownership, due diligence, exit planning, performance monitoring and regulatory evidence. A signed contract alone is not enough.

Professional services spend is difficult to compare

Consulting, legal, actuarial, audit, claims, marketing and advisory spend is often scoped differently by supplier. Procurement must define statements of work, rate cards, deliverables and acceptance criteria.

Contract obligations are not always operationalised

Audit rights, incident notification, data handling, subcontracting, service levels, termination rights and regulatory cooperation must be tracked after signature. Otherwise negotiated protections remain unused.

Supplier concentration and resilience risk can be hidden

The organisation may depend heavily on a small number of technology, data or outsourcing providers. Procurement reporting must show concentration, criticality, exit risk and continuity evidence.

Workflow design

Operational workflows connect business demand with supplier control, evidence and financial discipline.

01

Supplier criticality assessment

Classify supplier impact on customers, operations, data, systems and regulatory obligations.

02

Risk and security review

Route privacy, cybersecurity, compliance and resilience checks before commitment.

03

Sourcing and SOW control

Standardise scope, deliverables, rate cards, acceptance and commercial comparison.

04

Contract obligation tracking

Track service levels, audit rights, subcontracting, exit, incident and data clauses.

05

Renewal and license governance

Review SaaS, cloud and service renewals before automatic commitment.

06

Ongoing supplier monitoring

Monitor performance, incidents, risk reviews, concentration and remediation actions.

Implementation priorities

Start with the highest-risk control gaps, then scale the operating model and automation.

First horizon

Stabilise

Confirm ownership, supplier scope, approval thresholds, mandatory evidence and the exceptions that must be controlled immediately.

Second horizon

Standardise

Apply consistent workflows across entities, sites, roles, suppliers and categories while removing local workarounds that create leakage.

Third horizon

Automate

Use ZAPRO workflows, reporting and exception handling to make the target operating model repeatable, measurable and easier for users.
